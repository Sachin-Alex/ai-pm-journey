Section 4 — Problem Statements

Problem Statement 1
When Gold added restaurant restrictions, raised minimum order values, and introduced fees the plan didn't cover- without clearly communicating the change, subscribers felt misled rather than informed and cancelled,  evidenced by 3/6 interviews (Rohan, Karthik, Vikram), all cancelling in the same Jul-Sep window, with Karthik reporting 4/6 of his own Gold-subscribing friends did the same.

Problem Statement 2
When ordering dinner between 7-9 PM in dense tech corridors (Whitefield, HSR, Koramangala), users experience 60-75 min deliveries against a 25-35 min promise , evidenced by 3/6 interviews (Rohan, Anjali, Vikram), two of whom now default to a competitor first and treat this app as backup only.

Problem Statement 3
When a first-time user's very first order arrives late (QuickBites data shows the 3 customers, who churned after exactly one order were each delivered 15 minutes past the promised time) they treat that single experience as representative of the app and don't return, and the standard recovery (a flat ₹50 credit) doesn't change that outcome. Evidenced by QuickBites churn data (0% churn among customers whose first order was on time vs. 21.7% among those whose first order was 15+ min late, n=46) and 2/6 interviews (Anjali, Vikram's cousin) independently describing the identical late-delivery sequence of events.
Problem Statement 4 
When walk-in demand surges on weekends, partner restaurants can't fulfill delivery volume and cancel 20-30% of orders, but the platform's binary online/offline toggle penalizes them with visibility and rating drops instead of solving the actual constraint,  evidenced by Meera's first-hand account, independently corroborated by Vikram naming the same restaurant as closed Sundays.

Section 6 — Chosen Problem + JTBD

My chosen problem
When a first-time user's very first order arrives late (QuickBites data shows the 3 customers, who churned after exactly one order were each delivered 15 minutes past the promised time) they treat that single experience as representative of the app and don't return, and the standard recovery (a flat ₹50 credit) doesn't change that outcome. Evidenced by QuickBites churn data (0% churn among customers whose first order was on time vs. 21.7% among those whose first order was 15+ min late, n=46) and 2/6 interviews (Anjali, Vikram's cousin) independently describing the identical late-delivery sequence of events.
Why this one?
It answers the question posed by the manager. Manager's brief was specifically "is this an acquisition problem or something else?" This problem statement is the answer. It shows people are getting in  fine (App Funnel checkout-to-order is 78–85%) but leaving after one bad experience. 
JTBD Statement
When I try a food delivery app for the first time, I want my order to arrive when it was promised, so I can trust this app enough to make it my default the next time I'm hungry.
User Story Version
As a time-constrained working professional, who has the option of multiple food-delivery applications, when I try a food delivery app for the first time, I want my application to help me make quick ordering decisions and also deliver my food on time so that it becomes my default choice for the next time I’m hungry.
